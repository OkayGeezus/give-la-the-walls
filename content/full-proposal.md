## Original Vision

**Campaign statement:** GIVE LOS ANGELES THE WALLS.

**Philosophy:**

- DEVELOP THE ARTISTS.
- GIVE THEM THE WALLS.
- BEAUTIFY LOS ANGELES.

**System:**

TALENT → TRAINING → MENTORSHIP → OPPORTUNITY → PUBLIC ART → CAREERS

Los Angeles has extraordinary artistic talent — especially within communities where graffiti, street art, lettering, illustration, music, fashion and design are already deeply embedded in the culture.

The opportunity is to create more pathways that turn that talent into professional experience, economic opportunity and public art.

**Meaning of “Give Los Angeles the Walls” (not anti-government):**

- Give young artists somewhere to develop.
- Give established artists meaningful commissions.
- Give emerging artists professional opportunities.
- Give communities culturally meaningful public art.
- Give overlooked walls a purpose beyond being repeatedly vandalized and painted over.

## Youth Artist Development

**Working title (original):** From Graffiti to Opportunity

**Framing:** Don’t suppress the talent. Develop it.

Some of the young people writing on Los Angeles walls already possess the beginnings of valuable creative skills: lettering, composition, color, illustration, style, fearlessness.

The challenge is that many young artists may never be shown how those abilities can become a legitimate profession.

Proposal: a city-supported mural and street-art development program connecting young Angelenos with professional muralists, graffiti artists, designers, illustrators and other creative professionals.

The goal isn’t simply to teach young people how to paint murals — **teach them how to become professional artists.**

**Tone rules for Phase 2:**

- Do not portray young artists as criminals.
- Do not assume participants are responsible for vandalism.
- Frame around artistic development, mentorship, workforce development, creative economic opportunity, public art, city beautification, cultural identity.

## Graffiti / Maintenance Discussion

### Redirect the Energy (archived section)

Los Angeles spends resources responding to graffiti and vandalism after it happens.

This initiative explores another part of the solution: invest in artistic talent before its only canvas is an unauthorized wall.

Not every participant will become a professional artist. Not every mural will eliminate vandalism.

But we can create more legitimate places, mentors and economic pathways for young people who already have the desire to create.

**Progression:**

REDIRECT THE ENERGY.  
DEVELOP THE ARTIST.  
CREATE OPPORTUNITY.  
BEAUTIFY THE CITY.

### Recurring cycle on some surfaces

GRAFFITI → PAINT OVER → GRAFFITI AGAIN → PAINT OVER AGAIN

### Important caveats (must remain in Phase 2)

- Do **not** promise murals will eliminate graffiti.
- Do **not** claim the program will definitively reduce crime or vandalism.
- Frame reduced vandalism / maintenance pressure as a goal/opportunity that can be evaluated.
- Explore whether culturally meaningful commissioned artwork, local artist involvement, community ownership, mentorship, and better use of these surfaces can reduce the recurring paint-over cycle while creating public value.

## LA84 → LA28

Use **1984 / LA84** and **2028 / LA28**. Never LA88.

The 1984 Olympic era helped establish an iconic chapter in Los Angeles mural history. Decades later, many of those works are no longer visible — painted over, deteriorated, peeled away, or otherwise disappeared. This is **not** framed as restoring the original murals; it is about a **new generation** of monumental public art on appropriate walls.

Los Angeles hosted the world in 1984. In 2028, the world comes back. That creates an opportunity to begin another chapter in the city’s public-art history — artwork created by artists from the communities surrounding these walls; not artwork imported into Los Angeles.

**Timeline:**

- **1984** — The legacy.  
- **2028** — The new canvas.  
- **Beyond** — A permanent creative pipeline for Los Angeles artists.

## Cultural Respect

The goal isn’t to pretend commissioned murals will eliminate graffiti.

The goal is to create artwork that communities recognize as authentic, give artists ownership in the spaces around them, and replace repeatedly painted-over surfaces with something culturally valuable.

When local artists help create the environment, there is an opportunity to build a different relationship between the wall and the community around it.

**Instead of asking:** How quickly can we paint over this wall?  
**Let’s also ask:** What could this wall become?

## Artist Sourcing

Los Angeles already has the artists: muralists, graffiti artists, illustrators, designers, painters, photographers, lettering artists, and emerging creatives who simply haven’t received their first major commission yet.

Find them. Commission them. Pair experienced artists with apprentices coming through the youth program. Pay local creative talent to transform Los Angeles.

## Community Engagement

Public art can do more than beautify a wall. It can create identity, pride, and — when we invest in the people creating it — opportunity.

Proof moment: Venice Beach mural celebrating the Los Angeles Dodgers’ second consecutive World Series championship (Guillermo helped manage and paint). People stopped, photographed the mural, and connected with the work.

## Future Program Structure

Two connected components as **one system**:

1. **From Graffiti to Opportunity / Develop the Artists** — youth pathways: mural art, street-art techniques, illustration, lettering, design, project execution, mentorship, portfolio, pricing, client communication, project management, business of becoming a professional artist.

2. **Reclaim the Walls** — commission LA artists on repeatedly vandalized / painted-over surfaces; pair established artists with emerging artists, assistants, and youth apprentices.

**Connect:** The wall becomes the classroom.
